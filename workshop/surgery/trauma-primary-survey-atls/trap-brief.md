# Trap brief: Trauma primary survey (ATLS)

Built from knowledge (no pattern.md exists for this module yet). Sources: the note's ⚠ Exam notes (E5 conflicts), the "Most common / Investigation of choice / Drug of choice" table, and the predictable wrong models a final-year student builds. Frequency fields are therefore knowledge-based, not recall counts.

## TR1
- trap: Haemorrhage is the leading preventable death, so control bleeding before securing the airway
- wrong_answer: "Control haemorrhage first (C before A)" offered as the first step of the primary survey
- where: "First priority in primary survey" stems; sequencing questions with a bleeding limb or pelvis in the stem
- error_type: E9
- why_tempting: Haemorrhage is the commonest preventable trauma death and the bleeding is visible; prehospital and military protocols (MARCH) do put bleeding first
- frequency: knowledge-based; very common in MCQs that mix ATLS with MARCH
- seen_at: none (knowledge)
- refuted_by: K2, K3, K114
- yield: high

## TR2
- trap: A patient who talks is fully safe for now, so the primary survey can stop there
- wrong_answer: "Patient is talking, so the airway is secure; proceed to secondary survey" or "talking means breathing and perfusion are adequate"
- where: vignettes of a conscious, speaking trauma patient with a clear head injury or chest wound
- error_type: E4
- why_tempting: Speech is the most reassuring bedside sign; the qualifier "for the moment" is easily dropped
- frequency: knowledge-based
- seen_at: none (knowledge)
- refuted_by: K4, K136
- yield: high

## TR3

## TR6

- error_type: E1
- why_tempting: Both are shock states in trauma and both cause hypotension; the learner anchors on "trauma plus low BP equals bleeding"
