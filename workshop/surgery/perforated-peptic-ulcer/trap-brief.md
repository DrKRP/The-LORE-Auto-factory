# Trap brief: Perforated peptic ulcer

Evidence basis: `evidence/surgery/perforated-peptic-ulcer/pattern.md` (P1–P12, all model-knowledge), the note's ⚠ Exam notes (K78–K83), the "Most common / Investigation of choice / Drug of choice" table (K65–K77), and the author's knowledge of NEET PG / INI-CET stems. Every `refuted_by` ID exists in `claim-bank.json`. Unverified claims (K35, K53, K56) are not used as the sole refuter of any branch trap.

## TR1
- trap: The commonest perforating site is the posterior wall of the duodenum, or the lesser curvature of the stomach
- wrong_answer: "Posterior wall of first part of duodenum" or "lesser curvature of stomach" offered as the commonest site of perforation
- where: "Most common site of peptic ulcer perforation" stems (P1); site-of-perforation vs site-of-bleeding comparisons
- error_type: E1
- why_tempting: Posterior duodenal ulcers are the ones that bleed, and the posterior wall feels like the "deep, protected" site; lesser-curvature ulcers are the textbook gastric ulcer site
- frequency: pattern-only (P1, P4)
- seen_at: model knowledge
- refuted_by: K1, K2, K65, K67
- yield: high

## TR2
- trap: Posterior duodenal ulcers perforate and erode the gastroduodenal artery in the process
- wrong_answer: Any other named artery (left gastric, splenic, superior mesenteric, right gastroepiploic) offered as the artery eroded by a posterior DU bleed
- where: "Which duodenal ulcer bleeds and which perforates" stems (P4); "artery involved in bleeding posterior DU" stems
- error_type: E10
- why_tempting: Several arteries lie near the lesser curve and pancreatic head; learners half-remember "gastric artery" and pick the wrong one
- frequency: pattern-only (P4)
- seen_at: model knowledge
- refuted_by: K2, K67
- yield: medium

## TR3
- trap: Pneumoperitoneum is always caused by a perforated peptic ulcer
- wrong_answer: "Perforated peptic ulcer" chosen as the single answer to a question about any free gas, when the stem gives a case where a different hollow viscus or a non-perforation cause is the real answer
- where: "Most common cause of pneumoperitoneum" stems (P5); vignettes with free gas plus a non-ulcer history
- error_type: E4
- why_tempting: The superlative "most common" is memorised as "the cause"; the ulcer is the single most frequent answer, so it gets generalised to every case
- frequency: pattern-only (P5)
- seen_at: model knowledge
- refuted_by: K3, K66, K38, K77
- yield: high

## TR4
- trap: Any gas under the right dome of the diaphragm is free intraperitoneal gas
- wrong_answer: "Pneumoperitoneum / perforation" read from a film showing colon between liver and diaphragm (Chilaiditi); equally, Rigler's and football signs confused with the pseudo sign
- where: "Pseudo-pneumoperitoneum sign" stems (P12); supine-film sign questions; erect-film images with subdiaphragmatic gas
- error_type: E1
- why_tempting: Gas under the right hemidiaphragm is the classic site of true free gas, so colonic gas in the same place looks identical
- frequency: pattern-only (P12)
- seen_at: model knowledge
- refuted_by: K38, K77, K37
- yield: high

## TR5
- trap: CT is the first investigation in suspected perforation
- wrong_answer: "CT abdomen" offered as the first investigation, or "erect chest X-ray" offered as the most sensitive test for free gas
- where: "First investigation" stems and "most sensitive / investigation of choice" stems (P2, table rows)
- error_type: E8
- why_tempting: CT is the most sensitive test, so learners carry "most sensitive" into "first"; the reverse error happens when "first" is read as "best"
- frequency: pattern-only (P2)
- seen_at: model knowledge
- refuted_by: K5, K68, K6, K69, K39
- yield: high

## TR6

- wrong_answer: Answering CT where the PYQ key expects erect chest X-ray for an initial-investigation stem; or answering erect chest X-ray where the current-practice stem asks for the most sensitive test
- where: P2 stems and any stem with "investigation of choice" in an exam-key frame
- error_type: E5
- why_tempting: The learner has memorised one answer from one source; the PYQ key and the modern test of choice differ
- frequency: pattern-only (P2); ⚠ Exam notes (K79)
- seen_at: model knowledge
- refuted_by: K79, K69

## TR7
- trap: A normal erect chest X-ray excludes perforation
- wrong_answer: "No perforation" or "discharge / no further imaging" chosen when the film shows no free gas in a patient with peritonitis
- where: vignettes with a clear film and ongoing peritonitis; "next step" stems after a negative X-ray
- error_type: E4
- why_tempting: A clear first film feels reassuring, and "free gas is found on X-ray" gets stretched into "no gas, no perforation"
- frequency: model knowledge (exam-note K80 flags it)
- seen_at: model knowledge
- refuted_by: K7, K80
- yield: high

## TR13
- trap: The PYQ-key operation name and the strictly correct operation name are the same
- wrong_answer: Learner who answers "Cellan-Jones repair" to a PYQ-style stem that expects "Graham's patch" is marked wrong, or the reverse: learner answers "Graham's patch" for a pedicled omental tongue and is pulled up by a current-source stem
- where: "Surgical treatment of perforated duodenal ulcer" stems (P3); table row K73
- error_type: E5
- why_tempting: Graham described a free omental plug while the pedicled patch is Cellan-Jones; PYQs use "Graham's patch" for both
- frequency: pattern-only (P3); ⚠ Exam notes (K78)
- seen_at: model knowledge
- refuted_by: K47, K48, K78
- yield: high

## TR16
- trap: Shoulder-tip pain or a tracked-fluid picture is linked to the wrong eponym
- wrong_answer: "Kehr's sign" (left shoulder-tip pain, which belongs to splenic rupture) offered as the sign of perforation peritonitis; or "Valentino's syndrome" linked to splenic or hepatic problems
- where: signs-of-perforation stems (P8); eponym-matching stems
- error_type: E10
- why_tempting: Shoulder-tip pain is a shared sign, and the eponym of the spleen is the one most often memorised
- frequency: pattern-only (P8); ⚠ Exam notes (K82)
- seen_at: model knowledge
- refuted_by: K82, K76, K26
- yield: medium
