# Trap brief: Gallstones and acute cholecystitis

Scope: 24 traps (20–25 target). Sources: `evidence/surgery/gallstones-and-acute-cholecystitis/pattern.md` (P1–P14), the note's ⚠ Exam notes (K114–K120), the note's Most common / Investigation / Drug of choice table (K100–K113), and model knowledge of NEET PG / INI-CET distractor patterns. Facts come only from the claim bank; traps are steering, not facts.

Yield count: high 20, medium 4, low 0.

---

## TR1
- trap: The most common type of gallstone is cholesterol (or "cholesterol and pigment") rather than mixed, as the PYQ key expects
- wrong_answer: "Cholesterol stones" offered as the most common type when the question is a PYQ-style MCQ with mixed stones as the key
- where: "Most common type of gallstone" stems; stone-type option lists (cholesterol / mixed / pigment / brown / black)
- error_type: E5
- why_tempting: Current texts and Western sources say cholesterol stones are the commonest, so a learner who reads widely picks the modern answer and loses the PYQ key
- frequency: pattern P1 (model knowledge)
- seen_at: pattern.md P1; note Most-common table row 1; K114
- refuted_by: K14, K15, K100, K114
- yield: high

## TR2
- trap: Ultrasound is the investigation of choice for acute cholecystitis, so it is also the answer to "most sensitive and specific" test
- wrong_answer: "Ultrasonography" offered for "most sensitive and specific test for acute cholecystitis"; "CT" offered as the initial test for suspected cholecystitis or stones
- where: "Investigation of choice" stems that say "most sensitive", "most specific" or "gold standard"; first-line vs confirmatory ladders
- error_type: E8
- why_tempting: Ultrasound is the first test for almost every gallbladder question and is the right answer to the "first / initial" version of the same stem
- frequency: pattern P2, P3 (model knowledge)
- seen_at: pattern.md P2, P3; K116 exam note; table rows "initial investigation" and "most sensitive and specific"
- refuted_by: K2, K3, K49, K50, K102, K103, K116
- yield: high

## TR3

- frequency: model knowledge (standard MCQ ladder)
- seen_at: note Most-common table row "Non-invasive investigation of choice for CBD stones"; K51–K53
- refuted_by: K48, K50, K51, K52, K53, K104

## TR4
- trap: Biliary colic is colicky, intermittent pain, so pain that lasts a day with fever still counts as biliary colic
- wrong_answer: "Intermittent, waves of pain" offered as the character of biliary colic; persistent pain with fever labelled "biliary colic"
- where: Clinical vignettes with RUQ pain, duration in hours, fever; "which is the feature of biliary colic" options
- error_type: E1
- why_tempting: The name says colic, and the textbook image of biliary colic is a wave of pain; the look-alike is acute cholecystitis, which also starts as RUQ pain
- frequency: model knowledge; pattern P12 family (symptom-feature stems)
- seen_at: K118 exam note; note Clinical features
- refuted_by: K11, K37, K38, K118
- yield: high

## TR5
- trap: Courvoisier's law applies to any palpable gallbladder, so a palpable gallbladder always means a malignant obstruction
- wrong_answer: "Malignant obstruction" offered for any palpable gallbladder; or "stones excluded" for a palpable gallbladder with no jaundice
- where: Jaundice vignettes with a palpable mass; "Courvoisier's law" option lists; eponym-matching stems (P12)
- error_type: E7
- why_tempting: The sign is learned as a one-line rule and the jaundice qualifier is dropped; a palpable gallbladder also appears in empyema and mucocele, which are not jaundice cases
- frequency: pattern P12 (model knowledge)
- seen_at: pattern.md P12; K119 exam note
- refuted_by: K9, K119
- yield: high

## TR6
