## R1
- fact: Tetralogy of Fallot is the most common cyanotic CHD, with a prevalence of 1 in 3,000 births, an incidence of 5 to 7 per 10,000 live births, and 5% to 7% of all CHD.
- quote: "TOF is the most common cyanotic CHD, with a nearly equal sex distribution, a prevalence of 1 out of 3,000 births," … "and an incidence of 5 to 7 out of 10,000 live births." … "TOF represents 5% to 7% of all CHD."
- source: StatPearls — Tetralogy of Fallot
- url: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK513288/
- tier: 1
- date: 2024-12-02
- note: Page has no "beyond the neonatal period" qualifier; nearest wording is "decreased postoperative mortality after the neonatal period."

## R2
- fact: In infants not diagnosed before birth, presentation depends on the degree of RVOT obstruction. Those with moderate obstruction may be asymptomatic apart from a loud murmur and may be referred to as "pink Tets".
- quote: "In those undiagnosed before birth, presentation depends on the degree of obstruction to the RV outflow." … "Those with moderate RVOTO may be asymptomatic at presentation, except for the presence of a loud murmur," … "and may be referred to as "pink Tets.""
- source: StatPearls — Tetralogy of Fallot (History and Physical)
- url: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK513288/
- tier: 1
- date: 2024-12-02

## R3
- fact: Without surgical intervention the most frequent causes of mortality include hypoxic spells (68%), cerebrovascular accidents (17%) and brain abscesses (13%).
- quote: "The most frequent causes of mortality in patients with no surgical intervention include hypoxic spells (68%)," … "cerebrovascular accidents (17%), and brain abscesses (13%)."
- source: StatPearls — Tetralogy of Fallot
- url: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK513288/
- tier: 1
- date: 2024-12-02
- note: "include" lists causes; the page does not say hypoxic spells is the single most frequent cause, only that it carries the highest percentage listed.

## R4

- quote: "These may include atrial septal defects, patent ductus arteriosus, supravalvar pulmonary stenosis," … "Classic tetralogy of Fallot (TOF) is a congenital heart defect (CHD) that is comprised of 4 anatomical alterations"

## R6
- fact: StatPearls links TOF with a syndrome or chromosomal abnormality in 20% to 25% of patients and with 22q11.2 microdeletion (40% when pulmonary valve atresia is present). It does not rank 22q11.2 deletion as the most common syndromic association and gives no overall percentage.
- quote: "The remaining 20% to 25% of patients with TOF have an associated syndrome or a chromosomal abnormality" … "When a patient with TOF presents with pulmonary valve atresia, the association with 22q11.2 microdeletion increases to 40%."

## R7
- fact: Pentalogy of Fallot comprises tetralogy of Fallot plus an atrial septal defect; the term is old and seldom used now.
- quote: "Pentalogy of Fallot – which comprises tetralogy of Fallot plus atrial septal defect – is an old term seldom used now."

- note: Same page: "If tetralogy of Fallot occurs with an atrial septal defect, code separately the two defects". It does not list RVH and does not mention PDA.

## R8
- fact: CDC describes TOF by four features: a hole between the ventricles, narrowing of the pulmonary valve and main pulmonary artery, an aortic valve receiving blood from both ventricles, and a thickened right ventricular wall. It mentions no ASD, PDA or left ventricular hypertrophy. Prevalence is about 1 in 2,041 US births.
- quote: "A hole in the wall between the two lower chambers, or ventricles, of the heart" … "A narrowing of the pulmonary valve and main pulmonary artery." … "The aortic valve, which leads to the aorta, is enlarged and receives blood from both ventricles." … "The muscular wall of the lower right chamber of the heart (right ventricle) is thicker than normal" … "About 1 in 2,041 (about 1,754) babies in the United States are born with tetralogy of Fallot each year."
- source: CDC — Tetralogy of Fallot (Congenital Heart Defects)
- url: https://www.cdc.gov/heart-defects/about/tetralogy-of-fallot.html
- tier: 1
- date: 2026-01-08 (date shown on page)
- note: 22q11.2 deletion and DiGeorge syndrome are not on the page.

## R9
- fact: A tier-2 imaging review describes the boot-shaped heart as due partly to upward displacement of the right ventricular apex from its hypertrophy, notes decreased pulmonary vascular markings on the radiograph, calls transthoracic echocardiography the cornerstone of TOF imaging, says cardiac CT delineates small vessels, collaterals and pulmonary arteries better than CMR, and says CMR has no ionising radiation and gives anatomical and functional information.
- quote: "classical "boot-shaped heart" explained by the narrowing of the mediastinum" … "upward displacement of the RV apex due to its hypertrophy" … "RV hypertrophy with decreased pulmonary vascular markings" … "Transthoracic echocardiography is the cornerstone of imaging in TOF both pre and postoperatively" … "Compared to CMR, CCT provides superior delineation of small vessels, aortopulmonary collaterals and pulmonary arteries" … "CMR has no ionizing radiation, can be performed irrespective of patient's body habitus or imaging windows" … "can demonstrate static images of cardiac and pericardiac anatomy with high spatial resolution" … "CMR provides objective, accurate and reproducible quantitative measurements of RV size and function"
- source: Apostolopoulou SC, Manginas A, Kelekis NL, Noutsias M. Cardiovascular imaging approach in pre and postoperative tetralogy of Fallot. BMC Cardiovasc Disord. 2019;19:7
- url: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6323806/
- tier: 2
- date: 2019 (publication year)
- note: Independent of Radiopaedia (different domain, different authors). Not on page: "pulmonary oligaemia" wording, echo limitations for extracardiac anomalies or peripheral pulmonary arteries, CT for palliative shunt patency, pulmonary artery diameter vs ascending aorta, poor outcome with unaddressed PA hypoplasia, infants not showing the boot shape.

- fact: A second tier-2 review gives TOF as the most prevalent cyanotic congenital cardiac abnormality, 3 per 10,000 live births and 5–10% of congenital cardiac defects, and lists its components as RVH, pulmonary stenosis, VSD and overriding aorta. It lists 22q11 microdeletions among TOF syndromes without ranking them.
- quote: "Tetralogy of Fallot (TOF) is the most prevalent cyanotic congenital cardiac abnormality" … "affecting 3 per 10,000 live births, and comprising 5–10% of all congenital heart abnormalities" … "right ventricular hypertrophy, pulmonary stenosis, ventricular septal defect, and overriding aorta" … "including 22q11 microdeletions, trisomy 21, Alagille's disorder"
- source: Althali NJ, Hentges KE. Genetic insights into non-syndromic Tetralogy of Fallot. Front Physiol. 2022
