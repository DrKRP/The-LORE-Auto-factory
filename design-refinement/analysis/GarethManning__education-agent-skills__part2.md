## 1. What it is
- A CC BY-SA library of 165 teacher-facing "skills": each SKILL.md is YAML metadata (evidence_strength, named sources, I/O schema, chaining) plus a copy-paste LLM prompt that makes a teacher-side AI design a lesson, task, rubric or protocol.
- Domain 20 (student-learning, Part 1) is the only student-facing runtime-AI domain. Everything in Part 2 is design-time advice for teachers, so the value to LORE is the pedagogical content in the prompts (principles, "CRITICAL PRINCIPLES" lists, self-checks), not the machinery.
- Cross-cutting design principles (docs/): every skill cites named research and rates evidence honestly (strong/moderate/emerging/original); a mandatory Known Limitations section; an explicit EXCLUSIONS list (learning styles, learning pyramid, Brain Gym, left/right brain, MI as teaching prescription, Bloom as quality hierarchy, growth-mindset slogans); output formats with a built-in "self-check before returning"; progressive disclosure (registry first, full skill on demand).

## 2. Transferable principles

### Discrimination and distractor design (variation theory, Montessori, CPA)
- P1 Variation sweep for look-alike pairs: for each pair the exam confuses, author four short beats in fixed order: contrast (same case, two labels shown side by side), separation (vary ONLY the critical feature, e.g. same finding, different cause), generalisation (critical feature fixed, surface details such as age or setting vary), fusion (two features vary, learner coordinates). The learner taps which label fits each; the tutor never states the rule before the learner has seen it vary. Less is more: 3–5 minimal examples beat many random ones. — source: skills/global-cross-cultural-pedagogies/variation-theory-task-designer/SKILL.md — evidence claimed: Marton & Booth 1997; Marton 2015; Lo 2012 ("it is the pattern of variation, not variety"); Gu, Huang & Marton 2004; Kullberg et al. 2017 (strength rated "strong"; skill itself notes it fits well-defined concepts, not open interpretation).
- P2 Isolate the difficulty in option sets: the options of one probe differ in exactly one critical feature, and irrelevant features (length, polish, order of mention) are held equal, so a learner's pick diagnoses one misconception. — source: skills/montessori-alternative-approaches/three-part-lesson-designer/SKILL.md ("isolation of the difficulty") and variation-theory-task-designer — evidence claimed: Lillard 2005 (links it to cognitive load); Marton 2015.
- P3 Name, recognise, recall ladder for terminology and classifications: Period 1 name the item and nothing else ("This is X"), Period 2 recognition ("tap the X": easy), Period 3 recall ("what is this called?": four buttons with plausible names). If P3 is missed, drop back to P2 for that item and retry later, instead of telling. Max 3 new items per run. Pre-authored hint ladder alternative that uses recognition before recall. — source: skills/montessori-alternative-approaches/three-part-lesson-designer/SKILL.md — evidence claimed: Séguin 1866; Lillard 2005/2006/2012 (programme-level evidence, not the lesson in isolation); recognition vs recall memory (Anderson & Bower 1972).
- P4 Concreteness fading with explicit bridges: mechanism segments run case or schematic first, then a simplified diagram-in-words, then the abstract rule, with a named bridge node between stages ("the shunt you just traced is this arrow; this is the rule"). A learner who errs at the abstract stage is routed back one stage, not forward. Stages cumulative, not replaced. — source: skills/global-cross-cultural-pedagogies/cpa-sequence-designer/SKILL.md — evidence claimed: Bruner 1966; Fyfe et al. 2014 (concreteness fading beat abstract-first, concrete-only and simultaneous presentations for transfer); Leong et al. 2015.
- P5 Option-length and polish balance: the correct option must not be systematically the longest, most hedged or most fluent. Fluency and confident wording are not credibility signals, so distractors are written to equal the key in polish. — source: skills/ai-literacy/ai-output-critical-audit-designer/SKILL.md ("fluency is not a credibility signal"; "absence of hedging is a red flag") — evidence claimed: Ennis standards; this is my adaptation to MCQ test-wiseness, the repo only states it about AI output.

### Clinical reasoning and reflection (ladder of inference, experiential cycle, historical thinking)
- P6 Observe, infer, conclude ladder for vignettes: present the vignette findings as observations only; learner taps which findings the tutor should "select"; then which interpretation each supports; then the conclusion. Each wrong rung is a named reasoning error (anchoring on one finding, premature closure, assuming a cause). Always show at least two alternative ladders that fit the same observed data. — source: skills/systems-thinking/ladder-of-inference-reflection/SKILL.md — evidence claimed: Argyris/Senge (original-practitioner level, no empirical trials cited); I rate it "emerging", useful as a structuring device, not evidence.
- P7 Structured reflection then generalise: after a case decision, do not move on. Three taps: what happened (pick the decisive finding), why (pick the mechanism), what principle does this illustrate (pick the general rule). Then a transfer case with changed surface features. Experience without reflection is only activity. — source: skills/environmental-experiential-learning/experiential-learning-cycle-designer/SKILL.md — evidence claimed: Dewey 1938; Boud, Keogh & Walker 1985; Hattie 2009 simulation d=0.33 (moderate, strongest with debrief).
- P8 Source clash as a diagnostic, not a problem: for PYQ-key vs current-source conflicts, give two sourced statements and ask which the learner would answer in the exam and which is true; contradictions are clues that demand explanation. Hardest of the historian heuristics, so it gets a dedicated node and is not assumed from single-source nodes. Prerequisites first: do not test a composite judgement before the component probes on that path. — source: skills/historical-thinking/corroboration-skill-builder/SKILL.md; historical-thinking-assessment-designer/SKILL.md — evidence claimed: Wineburg 1991; Wineburg & Martin 2004; Reisman 2012 (NO significant corroboration effect, attributed to little discussion); Wineburg, Smith & Breakstone 2018 (80% analysed documents in isolation).
- P9 Informative wrong answers and "mention vs interrogate" levels: each distractor maps to ONE distinct reasoning level (surface: restates or recalls; developing: notices but does not use; proficient: uses the feature to decide). The path therefore reveals the level of thinking, not just right/wrong. — source: skills/historical-thinking/historical-thinking-assessment-designer/SKILL.md — evidence claimed: Wiliam 2011 ("the wrong answer is as informative as the right"); Gottlieb & Wineburg 2012 (experts and novices cite sources equally often; difference is qualitative); HATs three-level scoring.

### Confidence, motivation, wellbeing
- P10 Engineered success start and one-step ramp: the first probe of each segment is virtually certain for a prepared learner, then difficulty rises one increment at a time, then the target. Feedback after success attributes to strategy ("you used the contrast we practised"), after a miss to strategy ("that route didn't work yet, try the other"), never to ability, never "amazing" on an easy item (credibility). Optional pre-written peer case ("a learner like you also picked X first, then ...") serves as vicarious experience. Normalise difficulty after a second miss ("this is the stage where most learners slip; that is the learning"). — source: skills/wellbeing-motivation-agency/self-efficacy-builder-sequence/SKILL.md — evidence claimed: Bandura 1977/1986/1997 (mastery experience strongest source, verbal persuasion weakest); Schunk & Pajares 2009; Hattie 2009 (d=0.92, strength "strong"); Dweck, with Sisk et al. 2018 cited as saying mindset interventions alone are weak.
- P11 Autonomy support AND structure, no extrinsic reward: every explanation node states why this matters for the exam (rationale), offers choice only among valid routes (Why? / Go deeper / Next / Skip ahead if sure), avoids controlling words ("you must", "you have to"), and uses no points, streaks or badges for tasks that are already worth doing. Rigour never drops for engagement. — source: skills/wellbeing-motivation-agency/motivation-diagnostic-task-redesign/SKILL.md — evidence claimed: Deci & Ryan 1985/2000; Jang, Reeve & Deci 2010 (autonomy support plus structure); Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999 (overjustification); Reeve 2009.
- P12 Challenge-skill balance with a clear goal and immediate feedback: each segment opens with a one-line goal ("By the end you can tell A from B in a vignette"); every tap yields feedback in the next node; the fast lane (too easy) and the hint ladder (too hard) are the two walls of the flow channel. No interruptions mid-segment: no pop-up meta-questions inside a run of probes. — source: skills/wellbeing-motivation-agency/flow-state-condition-designer/SKILL.md — evidence claimed: Csikszentmihalyi 1990; Shernoff et al. 2003 (boredom when challenge low, anxiety when high); Hattie & Donoghue 2016.
- P13 Safe, predictable pacing: show progress ("step 3 of 6"), keep the same node shape within a segment, no timers or speed pressure (anxiety is an efficacy killer), no public-style comparison, errors framed as "tempting" not "wrong". The repo's own trauma-informed and UDL material says these changes help all learners, so they are universal design, not an add-on. — source: skills/wellbeing-motivation-agency/trauma-informed-practice-designer/SKILL.md; skills/inclusive-design/udl-barrier-anticipator/SKILL.md ("assessment format barriers unrelated to the learning goal", timed tasks as engagement barriers) — evidence claimed: Perry 2006 (safe, relate, reason); Craig 2016; UDL rated "moderate" by the repo itself (quasi-experimental, Ok et al. 2017).
- P14 If-then plan at the end: the report finishes with 2–3 pre-written implementation intentions chosen from the learner's own error pattern; the learner taps the one they will adopt ("If a stem says NOT or EXCEPT, then I underline it before reading options"). The cue is a situation, not a feeling; the action is tiny. A WOOP-lite version (wish and obstacle preselected from the path, learner taps the plan) replaces free-text WOOP. — source: skills/wellbeing-motivation-agency/implementation-intention-designer/SKILL.md — evidence claimed: Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006 (d=0.65, 94 studies); Oettingen & Gollwitzer 2010; Bettinger et al. 2012.

### Language, assessment logic, honesty
- P15 Plain words, one term per concept, language is not the construct: keep Tier 1/2 words simple around Tier 3 content terms (which stay, bolded); never alternate synonyms for the same term ("conclusion/summary/write-up"); slow down instructions, not content; idioms and phrasal verbs out of tutor lines; reply buttons are complete reasoning sentences ("Although X, the evidence points to Y because Z") graded in difficulty, so a button encodes the thinking pattern, not just the answer. Many NEET PG candidates read in English as an additional language, so this is a core accessibility rule. — source: skills/eal-language-development/language-demand-analyser/SKILL.md; vocabulary-tiering-tool/SKILL.md; academic-language-sentence-frame-generator/SKILL.md — evidence claimed: Beck, McKeown & Kucan 2002/2013; Cummins BICS/CALP; Gibbons 2015 (frames work when they encode thinking structure); Zwiers 2014.
- P16 Honest, level-based reporting: report each objective on five levels with fixed meaning (No evidence / Emerging / Developing / Competent / Extending), where Competent IS success, Emerging is a legitimate start, Extending is rare and needs evidence beyond the target. Descriptors are work-facing ("shows ..."), short (word caps per level), and each level pairs with a next step. The report also states what the path cannot prove (a single correct tap may be a guess). — source: skills/original-frameworks/coherent-rubric-logic-builder/SKILL.md; docs/brief.md Known Limitations convention — evidence claimed: Burch 1970; Haring 1978 instructional hierarchy; Sadler 1989; Manning's own guide (original methodology, labelled "emerging", not peer reviewed).

## 3. Rejected
- Domains with no transfer to a tap-only exam-prep module (one line each): historical-thinking (content-specific: sourcing, contextualisation, close reading; only corroboration and assessment-design ideas kept above); montessori-alternative-approaches (prepared environment, mixed-age, uninterrupted work cycle are physical or social; only the three-period lesson kept); environmental-experiential-learning (outdoor, biophilic, service-learning, ecological anchors are physical; only the experiential cycle kept); global-cross-cultural (Reggio documentation, emergent projects, Ubuntu collective tasks, place-based and phenomenon anchors: group or place based); professional-learning (teacher PD, lesson study, coaching, observation: wrong audience); curriculum-alignment (crosswalk, band translator, KUD chart: curriculum-office tools); systems-thinking (iceberg, hexagon, agency circles, leverage, mental-model mapper: need free-form construction or group dialogue); most original-frameworks (regenerative, three horizons, place-based, dilemma navigation, decision wheel: programme-design frameworks).
- ai-literacy sequences (Socratic dialogue with an AI, expertise interrogation, prompt literacy, hallucination fact-check, disciplinary AI comparison): all require a live AI the learner queries and free-text prompts; breaks constraints 1 and 2. Only the "fluency is not credibility" idea survives (P5).
- Free-text production tasks: writing sentence frames, argument scaffolds, disciplinary writing, drafting WOOP, self-explanation in words, co-constructing rubrics, learner-written attribution. Replaced by pick-the-best-sentence buttons (P15).
- Teacher-delivered moves with no button equivalent: teacher scripts, proximity and tone, co-regulation, restorative circles, RULER mood meter conversations, peer discussion and group roles.
- Peer modelling by a real peer (needs people): kept only as a pre-written fictional peer case (P10).
- Growth-mindset slogans and praise: the repo's own EXCLUSIONS.md and self-efficacy skill call this weak; not adopted.
- Learning-style, pyramid, left/right brain, MI-as-prescription: excluded by the repo itself; LORE should ban them in report and notes text too.
- Skill-chaining orchestrators, registry, MCP, context engine (Layer 2/3): runtime personalisation from student data; LORE does the equivalent deterministically from path.

## 4. Concrete changes for the LORE factory

**reference/techniques.md (new rows and recipes; IDs continue after T12)**
- T13 Variation sweep (P1): fits diagnosis, clinical, traps, mechanism. Recipe: 4 beats contrast, separation, generalisation, fusion, each a 2–3 button probe; minimal change between beats; rule stated only after beat 3.
- T14 Name-recognise-recall (P3): fits retrieve, consolidate, hook. Recipe: ≤3 items; name, tap-to-recognise, tap-to-recall; failed recall drops to recognition and returns later.
- T15 Observe-infer-conclude (P6): fits clinical, diagnosis. Recipe: three rungs; distractors are named reasoning errors; closes with "what else fits these findings?" (two alternative ladders).
- T16 Concreteness fade (P4): fits mechanism, management. Recipe: case, schematic in words, abstract rule, bridge nodes between; wrong at abstract routes back one stage.
- T17 Source clash (P8): fits traps, consolidate. Recipe: two sourced statements (PYQ key vs current source), "which would you tick in the exam, which is true, why do they differ".
- T18 Reflect then generalise (P7): fits clinical, management, diagnosis. Recipe: after the case call, three reflection taps (decisive finding, mechanism, principle), then a transfer case with new surface.
- Edit T4 Productive failure and T9 Retrieval gate recipes: first probe of a segment must be answerable by a prepared learner (P10); add a goal line (P12).

**reference/pedagogy.md (rules)**
- §3: distractor sets differ in one critical feature; the key is never systematically the longest or most hedged option (P2, P5). Each distractor is tagged with a reasoning level (surface / developing / proficient) in the blueprint (P9).
- §3: feedback language rule: attribute to strategy, never ability; no "great/amazing" on easy items; say "tempting" not "wrong" (P10, P13). After the second miss on a key probe add one normalising line.
- New §4a Motivation and safety: each segment opens with a goal line and a one-line why-it-matters-for-the-exam (P11, P12); no controlling words; no points, streaks or badges; no timers or speed cues; progress shown as "step n of N" (P13).
- New §4b Language: plain words, one term per concept for the whole module, Tier 3 terms bolded, no idioms or phrasal verbs in tutor lines, reply buttons written as complete reasoning sentences (P15).
- §5: allow an optional Reflect step after every case segment (P7); when the architect lists a look-alike pair in the blueprint it gets a T13 beat set (P1).
- §1: PYQ-vs-source conflicts use T17, never a bare statement (P8).
- New "banned claims" line: no learning styles, pyramids, brain-hemisphere or growth-mindset slogans in notes or report (EXCLUSIONS).

**reference/rubric.md (criteria)**
- R2 anchor 5 add: "wrong options in a probe differ from the key in one critical feature; each maps to one reasoning level; look-alike pairs get a variation sweep".
- R4 anchor 5 add: "one term per concept throughout; plain-word tutor lines".
- New R9 Feedback tone and efficacy, weight 1: 1 = praise on ability, "wrong!", controlling words; 3 = neutral, specific; 5 = strategy attribution, normalises difficulty, progress and goal visible, nothing patronising. (Could fold into R8, but separating it lets judges disagree usefully.)
- New R10 Transfer and reflection, weight 2 (optional if judges are overloaded): 5 = every key idea is reflected on (what, why, principle) and re-tested with changed surface features.
- New Gate G4 Banned content: learning-style claims, ability praise, countdown timers, points/badges in any node.

**scripts validate/factory.py (countable checks)**
- Option-length balance: per probe, flag when the key is the longest option; per module, flag if the key is longest in > 40% of probes (P5).
- Controlling-language lint: count "you must", "you have to", "wrong!", "amazing", "genius" and similar; warn on any (P10, P11).
- Goal line: first message of each segment matches a flag `goal: true`; fail if missing (P12). Rationale: ≥ 1 node per segment with `why: true` (P11).
- Term consistency: a glossary in the blueprint of preferred term per concept; flag any listed synonym appearing in tutor text (P15). Sentence length cap (e.g. mean ≤ 16 words) and idiom list.
- Variation sweep completeness: any node group tagged `technique: T13` must contain four beats in order and the separation beat must have options differing in one tagged feature (P1).
- Prerequisite order: for any composite probe tagged `requires: [probeIds]`, on every path the required probes appear first (reuses the all-paths walker already used for the objective guarantee) (P8, P16).
- Reflection coverage: share of case probes followed by a reflect triple; report the number (P7). Transfer: each key objective has a re-test probe tagged `surface: changed` (P7).
- Alt-representation option: each mechanism segment has a "show it another way" branch (schematic in words) (UDL representation, P4/P13). Count it; warn at zero.
- No-timer lint: reject `timer`, `countdown`, `seconds` in node text.

**Report rules (what the end-of-module report says for which path)**
- Per objective level (P16), derived only from path:
  - Extending: fast-lane probe correct and sure, AND transfer or delayed re-test correct with changed surface.
  - Competent: correct first try or after one hint, AND delayed re-test correct.
  - Developing: correct only after the explanation, but re-test correct; or correct first but re-test wrong.
  - Emerging: re-test wrong, or explanation taken twice. Say "a legitimate starting point" and give the specific next step.
  - Not seen: objective only in an unvisited `deeper` branch; say so, do not imply mastery.
- Reasoning level (P9): count surface / developing / proficient distractor picks and say which kind of thinking the learner tends to use ("you often picked the option that matches a single finding").
- Strategy attribution lines (P10): "You got X after the hint, so using hints works for you here" built from the `hint_used` and `retry` flags; never "you are good at".
- Calibration (hypercorrection): sure-and-wrong count named by misconception, with the one-line corrected statement and its claim IDs; sure-and-right count praised factually.
- If-then plan (P14): 2–3 plans picked by the dominant error pattern; learner taps one in the last node; the report repeats it.
- Honesty line (P16): "One correct tap can be a guess; Competent here needs a correct delayed re-test." No learning-style labels, no scores framed as ability.

## 5. Testable hypotheses
- H1 (P1, T13): segments authored with a variation sweep for a look-alike pair will score higher on R2 (misconception work) and give higher simulated-learner accuracy on the delayed re-test of that pair than the same pair taught with T7 contrasting cases alone. Measure: R2 judge score; sim re-test accuracy on the look-alike probe.
- H2 (P10, P13): segments whose feedback uses strategy attribution, a one-step ramp and a normalising line after the second miss will show a higher simulated-learner retry-and-continue rate after a first miss, and higher R8/R9 scores, than segments with generic correct/incorrect feedback. Measure: sim persona "discouraged" completion rate; R8/R9.
- H3 (P15): modules passing the term-consistency and plain-word lint will score higher on R4 and give higher accuracy for a simulated low-English-proficiency persona than modules that fail it, holding content equal. Measure: R4; accuracy of the EAL sim persona.
- H4 (P6, T15): diagnosis segments authored as observe-infer-conclude will cut simulated-learner anchoring and premature-closure errors (the distractors tagged as such) relative to case-first T2, without lowering interactivity. Measure: per-distractor pick rate in sims; R1 and R7 judge scores.
