# GarethManning/education-agent-skills — Part 1 analysis

Scope: domains student-learning, ai-learning-science, memory-learning-science, explicit-instruction, questioning-discussion, self-regulated-learning, curriculum-assessment (57 SKILL.md files; ~25 read closely or section-sampled, listed under "Files read"). Other domains (Part 2) not covered.

## 1. What it is

- A library of ~165 education "skills": each SKILL.md is YAML metadata plus a system prompt for a free-text AI coach or a teacher-facing design generator, with an Evidence Foundation paragraph, an example transcript and Known Limitations.
- student-learning is a set of learner-facing gates (retrieve first, explain first, hint ladder, diagnose the error, calibrate confidence, fade scaffolds, unassisted checkpoint, transfer bridge). Each writes an "evidence_captured" record (hint level reached, confidence before/after, error type, assistance tag).
- Nearly all runtime behaviour assumes the learner types and an LLM judges and responds. The reusable value is the design logic and the research each skill cites. Evidence quality is mixed and labelled by the repo itself ("strong", "moderate"). Citations are as claimed in the repo and were not independently verified here.

## 2. Transferable principles

- P1 Distractor = a named misconception with a diagnostic key: every wrong option is the answer a learner holding one specific misconception would pick, and the correct option cannot be reached by flawed reasoning. In the module JSON each wrong choice carries `misconception: "M<n>"`, and the blueprint records "what choosing this reveals". Add a check that no wrong option is also the right answer to a different question on the same stem (e.g. mean vs median vs range vs sum, all computed from the same data). The data must be chosen so the misconception answers differ from the correct one. — source: skills/questioning-discussion/hinge-question-designer/SKILL.md — evidence claimed: Wiliam 2011; Christodoulou 2017; Black & Wiliam 1998; Haladyna et al. 2002 (item-writing rules: parallel options, no "all/none of the above").
- P2 Option hygiene for button choices: options parallel in length and grammar, no "all of the above", no giveaway (longest is right), and avoid negative stems unless the exam itself uses them. LORE's "which is NOT" traps are an exam-format exception, so mark them `trap_format` and handle them deliberately. — source: hinge-question-designer/SKILL.md — evidence claimed: Haladyna et al. 2002.
- P3 Pre-written decision rule per response pattern: every probe states in the blueprint what each tap means and what happens next (proceed, targeted refutation, or re-teach). Assessment that does not change the next node is just a test. Feedback nodes should differ by misconception, never one shared "incorrect" node. — source: skills/ai-learning-science/formative-assessment-loop-designer/SKILL.md and hinge-question-designer/SKILL.md — evidence claimed: Black & Wiliam 1998 (0.40–0.70); Wiliam 2011; VanLehn 2006 inner vs outer loop (step-level feedback beat final-answer-only).
- P4 Pre-authored hint ladder with fixed rungs: conceptual question, then analogy or link to known case, then name the principle, then procedural nudge, then a parallel worked example with different surface content. Each rung does ONE thing, none gives the answer, and the bottom-out reveals the answer together with a self-explanation pick ("which reason explains it?"). Button form: the probe's `help` choice leads to hint1; hint1 offers "Try again" or "Another hint", and so on. LORE currently has one hint then explanation, so extend this to 2–3 rungs on key probes. — source: skills/student-learning/progressive-hint-ladder/SKILL.md; skills/ai-learning-science/adaptive-hint-sequence-designer/SKILL.md — evidence claimed: VanLehn 2011 (ITS d=0.76, human tutors 0.79, outer-loop only 0.31); Aleven & Koedinger 2002; Koedinger & Aleven 2007 assistance dilemma; Wood, Bruner & Ross 1976.
- P5 Try-first before hint: hints are reachable only after one committed attempt, because requesting help before trying is the documented misuse pattern. In buttons, the probe may offer "Not sure, give me a hint", but its first hint is a question that restates what the stem gives and asks for. The path records `hint_before_attempt` as a report signal. — source: progressive-hint-ladder/SKILL.md (warm-start protocol: list what you have, what is asked, which rule links them); adaptive-hint-sequence-designer/SKILL.md — evidence claimed: Aleven & Koedinger 2002 (hint abuse, "try first, then hint, then explain the hint to yourself"); Razzaq & Heffernan 2010 (proactive hints help weaker, reactive suit stronger).
- P6 Bottom-out with generation: when the ladder is exhausted, show the answer and its reason, then require one self-explanation tap, then re-test the same idea later in a different form. Never end on "the answer is X". — source: adaptive-hint-sequence-designer/SKILL.md (bottom-out strategy); progressive-hint-ladder/SKILL.md (level reached is evidence, not a grade) — evidence claimed: Aleven & Koedinger 2002; Hausmann & VanLehn 2007 (generation helps even when imperfect).
- P7 Diagnose-the-error taxonomy as choices: after a miss, instead of free-text diagnosis, ask a one-tap "What went wrong?" (I did not know the concept / I knew it, slipped in the step / I picked the wrong approach / I misread the question). Feedback then branches by error type: conceptual → refutation with a different representation; procedural → worked step; careless → a verify-habit tip; strategic → contrast of when each approach applies. The self-diagnosis accuracy is computable because the author knows the true type of each distractor. — source: skills/student-learning/stuck-and-error-diagnosis-coach/SKILL.md; skills/self-regulated-learning/error-analysis-protocol/SKILL.md — evidence claimed: Hattie & Timperley 2007 (process and self-regulation feedback beat task-only); Tulis et al. 2016 (detect, attribute, correct); Metcalfe 2017 (errors help only if analysed); Siegler 2002; Ohlsson 1996.
- P8 Cognitive conflict for entrenched misconceptions: elicit the belief with a prediction and a commitment (confidence tap), present the contradicting case, ask the learner to pick the reconciliation, then name the distinction. "Elicit then explain" beats explaining first. Errors that are logical within a wrong mental model need conflict, not correction. — source: skills/ai-learning-science/intelligent-tutoring-dialogue-designer/SKILL.md (Elicit, Conflict, Construct phases; gravity vs air resistance example) — evidence claimed: Chi et al. 2001; Chi & Wylie 2014 ICAP; Graesser et al. 2005 AutoTutor; VanLehn 2011.
- P9 Feedback quality checklist, usable as a rubric and validator target: (a) says which part is right and why (task), (b) names the process or why it fails (process), (c) gives a next step or check habit (feed-forward), (d) no self-level praise ("Great!", "Good effort"), (e) specific enough to act on, (f) novices get one or two points, not everything. Elaborated feedback for conceptual errors, bare verification for slips. — source: skills/ai-learning-science/ai-feedback-design-principles/SKILL.md; skills/memory-learning-science/feedback-quality-analyser/SKILL.md — evidence claimed: Hattie & Timperley 2007 (feed up, feed back, feed forward; mean 0.73 but variable); Shute 2008; Narciss 2008; Kluger & DeNisi 1996 (self-directed feedback can lower performance); Butler 1988 (a grade alongside comments suppresses the comments).
- P10 Confidence as report evidence, not conversation: key probes record Sure/Guessing, and the report names the pattern in plain words: confident-wrong ("high confidence, wrong: look again"), guess-right ("right but guessing: not yet secure"), well-calibrated, underconfident (low confidence, right). The repo's 0–100 slider becomes the existing two-tap. The "Sure but wrong" node also tells the learner that re-reading feels like knowing (illusion of competence). — source: skills/student-learning/confidence-calibration-check/SKILL.md; skills/ai-learning-science/metacognitive-monitoring-ai-contexts/SKILL.md — evidence claimed: Bjork & Bjork 2011; Koriat & Bjork 2005; Thiede et al. 2003; Hacker et al. 2008 (predict then compare improved later scores); Dunning & Kruger 1999 (weak evidence for the mechanism).
- P11 Retrieve-first gate with a gap map: before teaching a sub-idea, ask the learner to pick the correct statement among misconception foils (recognition, since typing is excluded), then teach ONLY the gap. A fully correct, sure learner skips; a wrong one gets the segment. The repo prefers free recall (stronger), so offer "build the answer" by tapping ordered or selected chips where possible (T12 style) to approximate generation. — source: skills/student-learning/retrieve-first-gate/SKILL.md; skills/memory-learning-science/retrieval-practice-generator/SKILL.md — evidence claimed: Roediger & Karpicke 2006; Rowland 2014 (d=0.50, 159 studies); Dunlosky et al. 2013 (practice testing "high utility"); Bjork desirable difficulties.
- P12 Retrieval types and misconception items: mix cued recall (scenario cue) and recognition; include at least 2 questions built to surface each known misconception; include an error-correction item ("this statement is wrong, fix it" as pick-the-correction). Calibrate to delay: more cued recall when recent, free-recall-like items when older. — source: retrieval-practice-generator/SKILL.md — evidence claimed: Roediger & Karpicke 2006; Karpicke & Roediger 2008.
- P13 Worked example, annotate the why, then backward-fade, performance-triggered: full example with reasoning per step, then the last step left to the learner, then the last two, and so on. A "common error" annotation sits at the step where it happens. Fading trigger in buttons = a correct (and sure) tap on the previous stage; a miss repeats the previous stage with different numbers. Do not fade on a fixed schedule. Fully worked steps on novices, faster fade for experts (expertise reversal). — source: skills/memory-learning-science/worked-example-fading-designer/SKILL.md; skills/ai-learning-science/worked-example-to-problem-solving-transition-designer/SKILL.md — evidence claimed: Sweller & Cooper 1985; Renkl & Atkinson 2003 (backward fading); Renkl 2014; van Merriënboer & Kirschner 2018; Kalyuga (expertise reversal); the repo warns that faded success can create an illusion of competence, so finish with a fully independent item.
- P14 Erroneous example after correct examples only: show a worked case or peer answer with ONE realistic error, ask "which step is wrong?" (tap a step), then "why is it wrong?" (pick among explanations, one of which is the real misconception), then the correct version. Place after a correct worked example, never as first exposure. Learning effect may be delayed, so the delayed re-test matters. — source: skills/ai-learning-science/erroneous-example-designer/SKILL.md — evidence claimed: McLaren, Adams & Mayer 2012 and 2015 (delayed benefit, best with self-explanation); Große & Renkl 2007 (needs prior knowledge, else the error can be learned as correct); Tsovaltzi et al. 2010; Siegler 2002.
- P15 Self-explanation pick at points of conceptual density only (new principle, non-obvious step, likely misunderstanding), written as explain-WHY not describe-WHAT, answerable from what has been shown. Three options: the real mechanism plus two plausible wrong mechanisms. Not at every step. — source: skills/ai-learning-science/self-explanation-prompt-designer/SKILL.md — evidence claimed: Chi et al. 1989; Wylie & Chi 2014; Hausmann & VanLehn 2007. The repo says generation beats multiple choice, so picking is a weaker proxy; keep it but pair with P11 chips where feasible.
- P16 Productive-failure shape with a short, bounded attempt: one problem slightly beyond the learner, two different attempts offered as "which approach would you try?" taps (two rounds with different approach sets), then consolidation that quotes what their choices missed ("You tried X; it ignores Y"). Not for novices who lack the prerequisites. The repo says "attempt first" gains show on conceptual and transfer measures, not procedural accuracy. — source: skills/student-learning/productive-failure-protocol/SKILL.md; skills/ai-learning-science/productive-failure-desirable-difficulty-designer/SKILL.md — evidence claimed: Kapur 2008, 2014, 2016; Sinha & Kapur 2021 meta-analysis; Loibl & Rummel 2014. Contested elsewhere, so treat as a hypothesis (H3).
- P17 Transfer bridge after success: near transfer (same principle, new surface) then far transfer (different context, same structure), each followed by three taps: what is the same, what is different, which principle travels. Near-transfer success with far-transfer failure is a named report pattern ("learned in context, not abstractly"). — source: skills/student-learning/transfer-bridge/SKILL.md — evidence claimed: Gick & Holyoak 1983 (surface similarity blocks transfer; naming the principle helps); Perkins & Salomon 1992; Bransford & Schwartz 1999.
- P18 Unassisted checkpoint tagged separately from assisted performance: the end-of-module check item must be answerable with no hint, no retry and no help choice, and the report separates "with hints" from "unassisted" results. If a learner scored well only on hinted nodes, say so. — source: skills/student-learning/unassisted-evidence-checkpoint/SKILL.md — evidence claimed: Bastani et al. 2025 PNAS (n≈1000; unguarded GPT-4 +48% in practice but −17% unassisted; hint-ladder GPT Tutor +127% practice, no unassisted difference). The repo itself says the checkpoint protocol was not tested; only the assisted vs unassisted distinction is supported.
- P19 Transparent fading of support as a path rule: scaffold levels 4 to 0 (full hints, up to principle-reminder only, diagnosis only, none). Within a module: segments later in the path offer fewer help choices once the learner has shown correct-first-try twice; earlier misses restore a rung. Across modules the repo tracks sessions, which a stateless static app cannot do, so keep this within-module and use the report to recommend what to do next. — source: skills/student-learning/fading-manager/SKILL.md — evidence claimed: Collins, Brown & Newman 1989; Wood, Bruner & Ross 1976; Pea 2004; Belland 2014 (fading is the most under-implemented part of scaffolding).
- P20 Checking-for-understanding stage map: hinge question after each explanation and before practice; the response tree distinguishes got it (proceed to interleaved practice), partial (one fix then re-check), not yet (re-teach with a different worked example). The "different example" rule matches LORE's "second explanation differs". — source: skills/explicit-instruction/checking-for-understanding-protocol-designer/SKILL.md — evidence claimed: Rosenshine 2012; Wiliam 2011; Christodoulou 2017.
- P21 Spaced review-card schedule with expanding gaps and a mixed retrieve: first review 1–3 days, then 1–2 weeks, then 3–4 weeks; review items are retrieval, never re-reading; mix topics. Matches the existing `schedule_days: [1,3,7,21]`. Interleave cards from different misconceptions rather than blocking. — source: skills/memory-learning-science/spaced-practice-scheduler/SKILL.md; interleaving-unit-planner — evidence claimed: Cepeda et al. 2006 (254 studies); Carpenter et al. 2012. The repo notes the evidence is mostly verbal-learning lab work.
- P22 Process-level statements in the report about help use: "level reached is evidence, not a grade". Report wording says what the hint level indicates and what to do next, without shaming. Pair with a "metacognitive prompt" tap at the end (what will you do differently?) chosen from pre-written strategy options tied to the error type. — source: progressive-hint-ladder/SKILL.md; stuck-and-error-diagnosis-coach/SKILL.md (retrospective question "how would you catch this next time?") — evidence claimed: Hattie & Timperley 2007 self-regulation level; Karabenick & Berger 2013 (adaptive help-seeking).

## 3. Rejected (need free text or runtime AI)

- Free-recall box with AI grading of accuracy/completeness (retrieve-first-gate, confidence-calibration-check): needs typed input and an AI judge. Approximated by P11/P12.
- 0–100 confidence slider and before/after comparison with AI naming the gap: replaced by two-tap Sure/Guessing and path-computed patterns (P10).
- Teach-back, explain-first interrogation and Socratic tutoring dialogue with open follow-ups (teach-back-evaluator, explain-first-interrogator, socratic-questioning-sequence-generator): rely on judging learner prose. Only the T1 ladder form of closed Socratic steps survives.
- Reflection required before each hint escalation ("what did that tell you?"): free text. A forced one-tap "which idea did that hint point to?" could replace it but adds friction; keep optional.
- Hint-clicking detection by time or rapid escalation: no timing data in a static deterministic player, though the app could record rungs used in sequence.
- Cross-session mastery tracking, fading criteria over 3 sessions, calibration trend across sessions (fading-manager, srl-session-wrapper, weekly-agency-review): needs persistent learner model across sessions. Only within-module versions kept.
- AI-generated problem for the unassisted checkpoint (generated at runtime): the item must be pre-authored.
- Dynamic adaptive difficulty selection and "silence is a move / thinking time" (intelligent-tutoring-dialogue-designer): runtime pacing by an AI; silence is not meaningful in a tap UI.
- AI claim checkers, hallucination protocols and AI-interrogation designs (ai-claim-checker, AI-literacy skills): not the product's concern.
- Open-ended design skills (project-brief-designer, curriculum crosswalks, rubrics, scope and sequence) and teacher-facing planning: not learner-facing conversations, and the claim bank governs content.
- Generation-over-recognition advice ("free-response beats multiple choice"): honest limitation of the button format. The factory should say so in the report (no claim of recall strength).

## 4. Concrete changes for the LORE factory

Techniques (`reference/techniques.md`):
- T13 Hint ladder (new row, fits: mechanism, diagnosis, management, retrieve). Recipe from P4–P6: on a key probe the `help` choice leads to hint1 (question), then hint2 (link to a known case), then hint3 (name the principle), and only then explain + self-explanation pick + later re-test. Max 3 rungs, each ≤ 40 words, each citing no more than the claims it needs.
- T14 Error-type diagnosis tap (new row, fits: diagnosis, management, traps). Recipe from P7: after a first miss, a 4-option "what went wrong" node routes to feedback tailored to concept / slip / approach / misread, then the retry.
- T15 Cognitive conflict (new row, fits: mechanism, clinical, traps). Recipe from P8: commit to a prediction with confidence, show the contradicting case (claim-backed), pick the reconciliation, then name the distinction. Extends T3 and T6; judge separately.
- T16 Near/far transfer bridge (new row, fits: consolidate, management). Recipe from P17: near item, far item (other module's claim via cross-module cite), three taps (same / different / principle).
- Amend T11: planted error appears only after a correct worked example in the same segment (P14); add the "which step" tap then "why" pick.
- Amend T5: backward fade, trigger = correct and sure; the last stage must be a fully unassisted item (P13, P18).
- Amend T9: gap-map route: each recognition foil maps to one sub-idea; teach only the failed one (P11).

Pedagogy (`reference/pedagogy.md`):
- §3 add: a hint never states the answer; each hint rung does one thing; first miss on a key probe gets rung 1, then a retry (extends "hint ladder"); bottom-out must carry a self-explanation pick (P4, P6).
- §3 add: feedback on a wrong option has no self-level praise or scolding words ("Great", "Good effort", "Wrong", "Careless"), names the specific step or belief, and ends with a next step or a check habit (P9).
- §3 add: erroneous examples and peer answers come only after a correct example of the same procedure (P14).
- §3 add: a probe whose distractors are all "plausible but unrelated" is filler; each wrong option's explanation must name which question it would be the right answer to, or which belief it encodes (P1).
- §4 add: fading is performance-triggered inside a segment: two correct-first-try answers reduce help options; one miss restores a rung (P19).
- §4 add: one segment per module must contain an unassisted item (no `help`, no `retry`) so the report can separate hinted from unassisted (P18).

Rubric (`reference/rubric.md`):
- R2 Misconception work: add anchors for 5 — "each distractor has a diagnostic key, wrong-option feedback names the belief, the same belief is re-tested later in a new form" and for 1 — "wrong options are the right answer to no question" (P1, P3).
- New R9 Feedback quality (weight 2): 1 = "Wrong!"/praise/answer dump; 3 = says why wrong; 5 = tempting-then-refute, specific step, a next-step or check habit, no self-level praise (P9). Judges tick the six checks.
- R5 Adaptivity: replace the 5-anchor "hint ladder" wording with "2–3 rung ladder on key probes, different second explanation, error-type branch" (P4, P7).
- New R10 Metacognition (weight 1): confidence-after-miss notes and a closing check-habit tap are present (P10, P22).

Validator checks (`scripts/factory.py` / `scripts/validate.py`):
- count hint depth: max chain of `kind: help` nodes per probe (target 2–3 on probes flagged key); warn if a `help` node's text contains the correct label verbatim (answer leak).
- error if a `hint` node's `choices` lacks a forward route to a probe or explain node, or if the ladder is a dead end.
- distractor check: every `misconception` choice has an `M<n>` and a feedback node that cites ≥ 1 claim; warn on two options with identical `next` that carry different `M` ids (shared feedback = undifferentiated).
- label hygiene: warn when options differ in word-count by more than 2x, or contain "all of the above"/"none of the above" (P2).
- banned-phrase scan in feedback `say` text for self-level praise ("great job", "well done", "good effort", "wrong!", "careless") (P9).
- fading check: each segment has ≥ 1 probe with no `help` choice; the module has ≥ 1 end-of-module probe flagged `unassisted: true` (P18).
- erroneous-example sequencing: a node with role `case` flagged `erroneous: true` must be preceded on every path in the segment by a worked-example node (P14).
- delayed re-test: every misconception M has a second probe in a later segment (extends the existing coverage check to count ≥ 2 probes per M at different depths) (P3, P6).
- review cards: `schedule_days` expands (each gap ≥ prior gap) (P21).

Report rules (path-derived, no AI):
- Hinted vs unassisted: "You got X right with hints on [topic] and Y right with none." If hinted-correct ≥ 1 and unassisted wrong on the same M: "This one needs a cold retry tomorrow." (P18)
- Hint depth: rung reached 1 → "a nudge was enough"; rung 3+ → "you needed the principle named: first card to review is [card]." Always phrase level as evidence, not grade (P22).
- Calibration pattern from taps: Sure+wrong on M → list M first as "confident but wrong" with the correct card; Guess+right → "right but not yet secure"; Sure+right → skip-worthy; Guess+wrong → "known gap" (P10).
- Self-diagnosis: if the "what went wrong" tap disagrees with the authored true error type, say "you called it a slip, but the mistake was the idea itself" (P7).
- Transfer: near right, far wrong → "works on the cases you saw, not yet on new settings" and queue the far item as a card (P17).
- Fading: report the point where help options were removed and whether a rung was restored (P19).
- Next-step recommendation chosen by the dominant error type across M (concept → re-read the mechanism segment; slip → verify-habit; approach → contrast cards; misread → slow-read tip).

## 5. Testable hypotheses

- H1: Segments whose wrong-option feedback follows the P9 checklist (names the belief, specific step, next step, no self-level praise) will score higher on R2/R9 and on simulated-learner "understood why" ratings than segments with a generic one-line correction, when the same facts and distractors are used.
- H2: Key probes with a 3-rung hint ladder + bottom-out self-explanation (T13) will score higher on R5 and produce fewer second-miss paths in simulated learners than probes with a single hint then explanation, without lowering R1 (check that the ladder is not skipped through).
- H3: Segments authored with the T14 error-type tap will have higher "second attempt correct" rate for the simulated learner personas holding the matching misconception, while adding at most 1 node per miss path (R4 stays ≥ baseline); prediction not guaranteed since the self-diagnosis tap may feel like friction for the tired-learner persona.
- H4: Segments using cognitive conflict (T15) or erroneous example after a correct example (T11 amended) will score higher on R2 than refutation-only (T6) on entrenched exam-trap misconceptions, and the advantage will appear at the delayed re-test node rather than the immediate probe (McLaren: effects delayed).
