# Analysis: lowwwbank/anything-to-course

## 1. What it is
- A Claude skill (MIT) that turns source material into a self-study markdown course: capabilities, modules, lessons, answers files, review schedule.
- Its core is an 11-step "attempt-first" lesson flow, a 16-item anti-pattern list and a per-lesson author checklist, all tied to learning-science meta-analyses.
- It also has a "study mode" tutor protocol (retrieval first, confidence before feedback, calibration log). Most of that runs on free-text answers and an AI grader, so only the design rules transfer.

Files read: `SKILL.md`, `references/learning-science.md`, `practice-design.md`, `quality-rubrics.md`, `course-blueprint.md`, `study-mode.md`, `examples/example-course/sql-for-analysts/review-schedule.md`.

## 2. Transferable principles

- P1 Attempt before principle: open each idea with a short, low-stakes prediction tap that the learner can attempt on prior knowledge, then state the principle and tie it to what they tapped ("You picked X; X misses ..."). LORE already has T4 and ask-before-tell, so the change is that the explanation must reference the chosen option. — source: `references/course-blueprint.md` (lesson steps 2-3), `SKILL.md` Step 4 — evidence claimed: Productive failure g≈0.36 (Sinha & Kapur 2021); pretesting (Pan & Carpenter 2023); learning from errors (Metcalfe 2017). Caveat the repo gives: keep attempts short, instruction must follow promptly.
- P2 Method-not-named items: some probes show only the situation ("What is going on here?") and the learner taps which concept or tool applies, with the label hidden. Choosing the concept is a separate skill from applying it, and it is exactly the NEET PG stem style. — source: `references/practice-design.md` § "Tasks that don't name the method"; `quality-rubrics.md` checklist "task where the method is not named" — evidence claimed: transfer-appropriate processing; interleaving (Brunmair & Richter 2019, g≈0.42, material-dependent).
- P3 Debrief every strong distractor, not just the one picked: after a wrong tap, or at the segment's consolidation, show why each plausible wrong option is tempting and fails, so false statements do not gain familiarity. In buttons: a "Why were the others tempting?" `agency` branch, plus a pre-written all-distractors recap node in the traps segment. — source: `references/practice-design.md` § "Multiple choice done right"; `quality-rubrics.md` anti-pattern "Trap distractors that don't correspond to real mistakes" — evidence claimed: Roediger & Marsh 2005.
- P4 Feedback anatomy as a fixed template: (1) what was right in the pick, (2) the exact step or assumption that failed, (3) the cause of the error, (4) link to the principle, (5) next step (retry or variation), (6) one main correction only, (7) about the work, not the learner. Maps onto LORE's tempting, refute, right-with-claims. Adds items (1), (5) and (6). — source: `references/practice-design.md` § "Feedback structure" — evidence claimed: Wisniewski, Zierer & Hattie 2020 (d≈0.48, driven by information content); Shute 2008.
- P5 Corrected retry as a variation: after remediation, the re-test changes surface features (age, sex, setting, numbers) but not the tested idea. A literal repeat tests memory of the option position. LORE's delayed re-test should say "different vignette, same misconception". — source: `references/study-mode.md` item 5 "Variations, not repeats"; `course-blueprint.md` lesson step 10 — evidence claimed: transfer literature (general; no single study).
- P6 Contrast after every worked case: each explain or worked example ends with the transferable principle plus one look-alike where it does NOT apply. A single example with no contrast teaches surface details. Buttons: a "Spot the difference" probe right after the worked example (T7 embedded in T5). — source: `SKILL.md` Step 4 "Example → principle"; `course-blueprint.md` steps 4-5 — evidence claimed: concreteness fading (Fyfe et al. 2014).
- P7 Path-derived calibration log in the report: "sure + wrong" is the top-priority flag, "guessing + right" is a fragile-knowledge flag, and two spaced clean successes mean graduation. Ports directly to a deterministic report rule and to a review-card schedule. — source: `references/study-mode.md` § "The calibration log"; `examples/.../review-schedule.md` "Calibration log" — evidence claimed: Koriat & Bjork 2005 (illusions of competence).
- P8 Spaced review cards with retrieval-only prompts: each card is a question to answer from memory, never "reread". The module ships a fixed +1/+7/+21 day card order; for a "sure + wrong" topic, the card jumps to tomorrow. LORE already has `schedule_days: [1,3,7,21]`, so add the path-driven reordering. — source: `references/practice-design.md` § "Spacing and interleaving"; `course-blueprint.md` review-schedule section — evidence claimed: Dunlosky et al. 2013; Rawson & Dunlosky 2013 (successive relearning, retrieve to criterion).
- P9 First-use rule: a term the learner may not know is defined in plain words before it carries load in a question, option or feedback. Includes abbreviations, and "no new jargon inside options or feedback". For med students most terms are known, so apply it to eponyms, scoring systems and newly introduced abbreviations. — source: `SKILL.md` Step 4 "First-use rule"; `quality-rubrics.md` Explanation checklist — evidence claimed: pre-training principle (Mayer 2017); curse of knowledge (Shatz 2023).
- P10 Difficulty must come from the discrimination being trained, not from wording: no trick stems, no answer inferable from option length or grammar, no hidden requirements. A cheap lint on option labels. — source: `references/practice-design.md` § "Multiple choice done right" — evidence claimed: desirable-difficulty framing (Koriat & Bjork 2005).
- P11 Diagnostic opener picks support level, not a grade: a short routing probe at segment start routes to more or less scaffolding. Same as LORE's fast lane, with the extra rule that "supports are skippable, not removed" when unsure of level. — source: `SKILL.md` Step 2; `practice-design.md` § "Scaffolding and fading" — evidence claimed: expertise reversal meta-analysis 2025 (Learning and Instruction); van Gog, Paas & Sweller 2010.
- P12 Cumulative checkpoint with an integration item: a late segment mixes items from all earlier segments in shuffled order, including one stem that does not name the topic. Maps to the consolidate segment. — source: `references/course-blueprint.md` § "Checkpoint level" — evidence claimed: interleaving (Brunmair & Richter 2019).

## 3. Rejected

- Free recall before options ("formulate from memory, then see options"): needs typed or spoken learner output and no pre-authored check can verify it. Closest substitute: P2 plus a confidence tap before the options appear.
- Free-response completion and independent tasks, open cases graded against a six-dimension rubric: need free text and an AI or human grader.
- "AI as coach" section (AI cites fragments of the learner's answer, separates errors from defensible judgment calls): runtime AI grading is banned.
- Calibration log written to `study-log.md` and read by the tutor in later sessions: needs cross-session storage and a runtime agent. Only the path-derived in-report version (P7) survives.
- Self-explanation prompts answered in words: replaced by LORE's T8 pick-the-explanation, which already exists.
- Course-level artifacts (syllabus, overview files, answers/ folder for hiding feedback): the button format already hides feedback until after the tap.
- "Writing down your answer on paper" as a commitment device: not observable by the app.

## 5. Testable hypotheses

- H1: Segments in which each wrong pick gets its own explanation opener that references the pick (P1) will score higher than segments with a shared opener on R2 (misconception work), judged blind.
- H2: Modules containing unlabelled-situation picks (P2, T13) in the diagnosis and traps segments will score higher on R6 (exam alignment) and produce fewer simulated-learner errors on the final traps segment than modules whose stems name the diagnosis.
- H3: Re-tests authored as variations with different surface (P5) will show a lower simulated-learner "correct by position/pattern" rate (simulated learner answers the re-test right while missing the original twice) than literal repeats.
- H4: Reports that lead with a "sure and wrong" list and priority-ordered cards (P7, P8) will be rated more actionable by the learner-advocate judge than reports that list scores by segment.
