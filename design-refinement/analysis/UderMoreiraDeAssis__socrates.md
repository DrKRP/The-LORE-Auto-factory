# Analysis: UderMoreiraDeAssis/socrates

## 1. What it is
- A GPL-3.0 persona and protocol for "evidence-based Socratic tutoring" mirrored across 7 AI tools (Claude Code skill and subagent, Codex, OpenCode, Gemini, Copilot, Cursor, Windsurf).
- Protocol: diagnose (ZPD probe), contract (intensity: gentle/standard/adversarial), inquiry loop of one question per turn drawn from Paul & Elder's six question categories, a six-rung contingency ladder, then consolidation (free recall, gap repair, transfer, metacognitive close, spaced follow-up).
- The best-cited of the four repos: an annotated bibliography with about 20 sources. Almost all behaviour needs free-text answers and live adaptation, so what transfers is the question taxonomy, the contingency ladder expressed as fixed branches, and the stuck-protocol.

Files read: `.claude/skills/socratic-inquiry/SKILL.md`, `references/question-taxonomy.md`, `references/evidence-base.md`, `.claude/agents/socratic-tutor.md`, `README.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, `AGENTS.md`. The per-tool mirror files were not read (adapters of the same persona, per `AGENTS.md`).

## 2. Transferable principles

- P1 Contingency ladder as fixed branches: the six rungs map onto a probe's button outcomes. Correct and Sure: skip or harder probe (fade). Correct and Guessing: a "Why does that work?" pick-the-reason node. Partially correct: a boundary-case counterexample node. Wrong: a question whose honest answer exposes the contradiction. Stuck once: hint. Stuck twice: partial worked example, then full explanation, then a near-transfer probe. LORE has most rungs (hint ladder, fast lane, sure_note); the missing ones are "Correct but Guessing leads to self-explanation" and "post-explanation near-transfer probe". — source: `.claude/skills/socratic-inquiry/SKILL.md` Phase 3 rule 4; `references/question-taxonomy.md` § Contingency ladder — evidence claimed: Wood & Wood 1999 (contingent tutoring); Wood, Bruner & Ross 1976; Chi et al. 1994 (self-explanation); Kalyuga et al. 2003 (expertise reversal).
- P2 Wrong answer gets a contradiction-exposing question, not "wrong": the feedback node for a misconception pick poses a pick-one question whose honest answer shows the learner's claim fails ("If that were true, what would the saturation be after a spell?"), with 2-3 pre-written answers. It is an elenchus in buttons: tap, see the consequence, then the refutation. Stronger than telling when the misconception is mechanism-based. — source: `SKILL.md` Phase 3 rule 4 "Wrong"; `references/evidence-base.md` "Classical grounding" — evidence claimed: Lepper & Woolverton 2002 (expert tutors rarely say "wrong", protect motivation).
- P3 Stuck-protocol with a worked example before the full explanation: after two misses the sequence is hint, then partial worked example (last step left to the learner), then full explanation, then a near-transfer check. Adds the middle rung to LORE's "second miss gets the explanation". — source: `SKILL.md` Phase 3 rule 4 "Stuck"; `socratic-tutor.md` § Inquiry loop item 6 — evidence claimed: Kirschner, Sweller & Clark 2006 (unguided discovery fails novices); Mayer 2004.
- P4 Near-transfer probe after a full explanation: once the learner has been told the answer, the next node is one fresh probe to confirm uptake before moving on. In LORE the delayed re-test is later; this adds an immediate transfer check after the explanation branch only. — source: `SKILL.md` Phase 3 "Stuck" last clause; `socratic-tutor.md` item 6 — evidence claimed: Kirschner et al. 2006.
- P5 Question taxonomy as an authoring checklist: six categories (clarification, probing assumptions, probing reasons/evidence, viewpoints, implications/consequences, question-about-the-question) with stems. For a clinical module the useful ones: assumptions ("what are you taking for granted in this vignette?"), evidence ("which finding discriminates?"), implications ("if so, what do you expect on examination?"), viewpoints (the examiner's key versus the current guideline, which maps to the "PYQ says X; source says Y" rule). Authors tag each probe with a category; variants must span at least 3. — source: `references/question-taxonomy.md` — evidence claimed: Paul & Elder 2006; Anderson & Krathwohl 2001 (revised Bloom); Graesser & Person 1994 (deep questions correlate with learning gains).
- P6 Share of deep questions: at least 70% of questions after diagnostics are analyze/evaluate/create (why, how, what-if), not recall. Port as a count of probes tagged "recall" versus others per segment. — source: `SKILL.md` Phase 3 rule 2 — evidence claimed: Graesser & Person 1994. The 70% figure is the repo's own target, not a research finding.
- P7 One question per node: exactly one question per turn, no barrage. LORE's 3 messages per node can hold two questions. Rule: at most one message per node ends in a question mark. — source: `SKILL.md` Phase 3 rule 1 — evidence claimed: Graesser & Person 1994; Rowe 1986 (wait time); Sweller 1988.
- P8 Perspective shift once per module: adversary, another discipline, inversion, future-self. Medical equivalents: "the examiner's trap", "the same finding in a different diagnosis" (contrast), "what would have to be true for the opposite answer?" (inversion). A single inversion probe per module. — source: `SKILL.md` § Perspective-shift battery — evidence claimed: none specific.
- P9 Consolidation by free recall, translated: the "summarize the 3 key ideas" step becomes T12 (learner builds the summary by picking true statements or ordering), followed by gap repair, transfer, and a metacognitive close. Add the last two as fixed nodes: a transfer probe in a new surface and a one-tap metacognitive question ("Which idea would you re-check tomorrow?" with options listing the module's key ideas, whose choice feeds the report). — source: `SKILL.md` Phase 4 — evidence claimed: Roediger & Karpicke 2006; Flavell 1979; Zimmerman 2002; Cepeda et al. 2006.
- P10 Feedback answers three questions: where am I going, how is it going, what is next (task and process, never the person). Pre-author each feedback node's last line as the "what next" signpost ("Next: three look-alikes."). — source: `SKILL.md` Phase 3 rule 5 — evidence claimed: Hattie & Timperley 2007.
- P11 Intensity as a choice at the start (gentle / standard / adversarial): in buttons, an opening tap picks a pre-authored track (more scaffolding versus straight to hard probes). Works as a fixed fork at S1, but costs authoring volume; see hypothesis H3. — source: `SKILL.md` Phase 2 — evidence claimed: Kalyuga et al. 2003.

## 3. Rejected

- Phase 0 domain deep-dive with web search and a private concept map per learner request: runtime AI, and the factory already has source-first research and a blueprint.
- Free-form diagnostic ("What's your current picture of X?") and classification into novice/intermediate/advanced by reading the answer: needs free text and runtime judgment. Replaced by a tap-based diagnostic probe (P1 fade) plus confidence.
- "Never say wrong" implemented by generating a contradiction question live: the fixed-branch version (P2) is kept, the live generation is not.
- Contract negotiation ("Shall I begin or adjust focus?"): conversational; only the intensity fork (P11) survives.
- Free-recall synthesis ("without scrolling up, summarize"): needs typed output.
- Question stems like "What exactly do you mean by ...?" and "What evidence would change your mind?": ask the learner to articulate; they need free text. Only the buttonable categories (P5) are kept.
- "Direct answer now, or keep building it?" escape: a pre-authored "Just tell me" help button already exists in LORE (kind `help`).
- Spaced follow-up in 2-7 days suggested by the tutor: LORE has cards with `schedule_days`.
- Ready-made code-review and architecture playbooks: wrong domain for NEET PG.
- Persona mirrored across 7 tools: packaging, no pedagogy.

## 5. Testable hypotheses

- H1: Segments in which the second miss gets a partial worked example before the full explanation (P3) and a near-transfer probe after it (P4) will show a higher simulated-learner correct rate on the immediate transfer probe, and a higher R5 score, than segments with hint then full explanation only.
- H2: Segments where Correct+Guessing taps route to a "why does that work" pick (P1) will have fewer simulated "lucky-guess" learners wrong at the delayed re-test than segments where correct answers always go straight on.
- H3: A tap-chosen intensity track at the hook (P11) will not beat the single-track version on composite score once authoring effort is held constant, so is not worth the extra segments (this is a null-result experiment to avoid building it without evidence).
- H4: Segments whose probes cover three or more question types (P5) will score higher on R1 (learner does the thinking) than segments that are mostly recall, blind-judged by the learning-scientist lens.
