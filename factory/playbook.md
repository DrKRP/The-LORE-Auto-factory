# Playbook

What the factory has learned about teaching by conversation. `scripts/factory.py record` appends one line per segment tournament: segment type, winning technique, the techniques it beat, and the editor's lesson. The architect and authors read the recent lines; edit or condense freely, keeping the `- [type]` prefix so authors can find lines for their segment type.

## Starting principles
- [any] Distractors drawn from real PYQ keys beat invented ones: they are the errors learners actually make.
- [any] The first explanation that fails should be followed by a different kind of explanation, not the same one repeated.

## Lessons
- [hook] T9 over T4, T2 (paediatrics/tetralogy-of-fallot S1, merge): For a hook segment, the winner lets the learner fail a real exam stem first and responds with a tempting-then-nudge hint and a retry in a different form, not the answer; put any 'X vs Y' contrast the objective needs on the correct path too, not only on the remediation path.
- [clinical] T2 over T3, T7 (paediatrics/tetralogy-of-fallot S3, merge): For clinical-picture segments, the winner pairs each key vignette with a retry and a second explanation from a different angle, and ends on a multi-trap 'which does NOT fit' that re-tests every trap from the segment at once; check that each option's trap tag matches the trap map's wrong model.
- [diagnosis] T5 over T3, T1 (paediatrics/tetralogy-of-fallot S4, merge): For investigation segments, a faded worked example with a skip lane wins, but the 'why' (mechanism) probe must sit on the shared path before the fork so fast-laners still commit on the causal objective.
- [consolidate] T9 over T12, T8 (paediatrics/tetralogy-of-fallot S8, pick): For consolidation segments, a skip-if-correct fast lane plus a differently-explained rebuild and cold probes beats linear probe sets.
- [management] T5 over T2, T11 (paediatrics/tetralogy-of-fallot S5, pick): For management-sequence segments the variant that tests each step in two forms (first-step, re-asked after a miss, then a cold ordering probe) beats worked-example or error-spotting variants; fix tell runs and dominated distractors on merge.
- [mechanism] T1 over T8, T6 (paediatrics/tetralogy-of-fallot S2, merge): For a mechanism segment, a causal ladder with one committed idea per rung wins; graft a clinical vignette re-test and keep retry lines and hints from naming the answer.
- [management] T4 over T2, T8 (paediatrics/tetralogy-of-fallot S6, merge): For a PYQ-trap recall segment, the winner is the one with a distinct feedback node per distractor naming the wrong model; graft others' retests and gradient foils, and strip uncited 'why tempting' explanations.
- [traps] T6 over T11, T10 (paediatrics/tetralogy-of-fallot S7, pick): For exam-trap segments, a classmate-correction frame with named wrong models beats abstract drill lists, whose distractors are dominated and keys findable by form.
- [hook] T9 over T4, T2 (surgery/acute-intestinal-obstruction S1, merge): For a hook segment on exam-key vs current practice, a both-columns contrast probe plus a skip lane beats plain re-asking; give every trap probe a hint and retry.
- [clinical] T3 over T7, T6 (surgery/acute-intestinal-obstruction S2, merge): For reversed-mechanism segments the winner was the one with a real hint-then-retry ladder naming the tempting model; fixes such as key position, dominated options and a skip lane can be applied by the editor.
- [diagnosis] T2 over T11, T7 (surgery/acute-intestinal-obstruction S3, merge): For diagnosis segments, three-option probes with named tempting reasons in hints beat two-option probes; strip absolute words like 'only' from keys.
- [management] T5 over T1, T11 (surgery/acute-intestinal-obstruction S4, merge): For management segments, the variant that probes every objective with agency routes and a fast lane beats a tidier but narrower ladder; always re-tag untagged partial distractors to a named trap and keep true steps out of the wrong-answer slots.
- [traps] T2 over T11, T7 (surgery/acute-intestinal-obstruction S5, merge): For look-alike-pair segments, the best-scored item set still needs a pass for form cues (longest key, absolute-word distractors, key position) and for objective gaps; one eponym per probe teaches better than a matching item.
- [hook] T9 over T2, T4 (surgery/acute-appendicitis S1, merge): For a hook segment, a retrieval-gate spine with a skip lane wins, but every first miss still needs a nudge-only retry plus an optional 'Show me why', and every partial needs its own wrong model so no option is dominated.
- [consolidate] T8 over T10, T9 (surgery/acute-intestinal-obstruction S6, merge): For a final cold-check segment, take the entry with the most distinct trap-mapped distractors and turn every miss into hint-then-retry; form cues in the key (the lone option that splits cases) need a final pass.
- [mechanism] T7 over T4, T5 (surgery/acute-appendicitis S2, merge): For a mechanism segment, a probe-per-step spine with a skip lane wins, but each trap needs a second test in a reversed form, every first miss must be a nudge that does not state the key, and option wording must be length-matched so no key can be found by form.
- [clinical] T2 over T7, T6 (surgery/acute-appendicitis S3, merge): When two variants tie, take the one with the real clinical hook and cold close as the spine and graft the other's per-option hypercorrection notes and direction probe; give look-alike traps (signs sharing a limb) a probe in more than one form, and never let a hint name the key.

## Lessons from surgery/acute-intestinal-obstruction (2026-10-19)
- The final consolidate segment must be a short mixed quiz (8–10 new stems), not a replay of earlier items; sims of every persona flagged the replay as dead weight and it inflates "cold" recall in the report.
- Re-ask nodes (a reordered copy of a probe after "Try again") count as new probes in the report engine, so an immediate retry reads as recovery. Prefer "Try again" back to the same node; a later re-test in a new form is the recovery evidence.
- Skip buttons must land past the material they skip, or be labelled as a quick check.
- [diagnosis] T2 over T5, T11 (surgery/acute-appendicitis S4, merge): Probe-first with a skip lane and a two-form test of each trap beats worked-case-first for investigation segments; make sure every dominated distractor is rewritten, and write trap options in the same hedged register as the key.
- [management] T8 over T1, T11 (surgery/acute-appendicitis S5, merge): For management segments, one compact probe per decision with a nudge-then-Show-me-why path beats a long branching tree; rewrite dominated over-correction distractors into plausible hedged options.
- First tap on a trap option must refute it in one line (why it fails) before the hint / Try again; a hint that only re-asks leaves the belief uncorrected (misconceived sim, obstruction r1).
- [hook] T4 over T2, T9 (surgery/inguinal-hernia S1, merge): For a hook segment, a stem that gives only one landmark (no restated definition) makes the probe diagnostic; retest in a different form, and add a skip lane so strong learners are not made to sit through the lesson.
- [traps] T2 over T10, T7 (surgery/acute-appendicitis S6, merge): For a final cold-check segment, take the spine with the best trap coverage, rewrite any stem that replays earlier segments into a new surface form, give every option a matching clause so form does not leak the key, and keep hints only in the first half so the unaided check is real.
- [mechanism] T3 over T6, T5 (surgery/inguinal-hernia S2, merge): For a mechanism segment, keep predict-first probes as the spine and give experts a skip lane, rather than opening with explain nodes; make stems name the question, not the answer.
- [clinical] T7 over T6, T3 (surgery/inguinal-hernia S3, merge): When judges rank a fast-lane, hint-ladder spine first, graft predict-first openings and second-form retests onto it, and let neutral correct-answer feedback double as the Show me why target to keep node count down.
- [diagnosis] T2 over T11, T7 (surgery/inguinal-hernia S4, pick): When one variant already carries every trap with hint ladders and a fast lane, pick it and spend the edit on one-line refutations and length-balanced option labels rather than grafting more nodes.
- [management] T1 over T11, T4 (surgery/inguinal-hernia S5, merge): For a management-choice segment, take the probe-dense spine, open on a question rather than a worked tell, and make every second probe of a trap a different form (stem, which-step-wrong, definition pairing) with plausible wrong models instead of dominated ones.
- [hook] T9 over T2, T4 (surgery/burns-assessment-and-fluid-resuscitation S1, merge): For a retrieval-gate hook, meeting the same trap in three new forms (gate, delayed probe, cold final) with a skip lane beat variants with more explanation nodes; the work left is balancing option length.
- [consolidate] T8 over T10, T9 (surgery/inguinal-hernia S6, merge): For a cold retest segment, the winner is the one with one probe per trap and a hint-free second half, but only if every stem is rewritten in a new form; check each stem against earlier segments before accepting it.
- [diagnosis] T11 over T5, T3 (surgery/burns-assessment-and-fluid-resuscitation S2, merge): For a diagnosis-ladder segment, a which-step-wrong drill with two distinct wrong models per probe beat denser explanation, and the work left is balancing option length and key position.
- Every trap feedback must open by saying plainly the choice is not right; a "Tempting… look again" nudge alone leaves fast clickers unsure (guesser sim, appendicitis r1).
- Do not re-ask the same rule in several segments with the same stem; a rule met in S2 comes back only once, in a new form (all four appendicitis sims).
- [clinical] T3 over T6, T2 (surgery/burns-assessment-and-fluid-resuscitation S3, merge): For a recognise-then-treat segment, the spine that names each wrong model and explains why it tempts won; grafting a third look-alike option into binary probes and balancing option length is the remaining work.
- [clinical] T7 over T6, T2 (surgery/burns-assessment-and-fluid-resuscitation S4, merge): For a calculation segment, put the narrative stem first, keep feedback to the wrong model and the repair without revealing the number, and retest the recalled figures in a new calculation and a no-chart probe rather than in the feedback.
- [management] T4 over T8, T5 (surgery/burns-assessment-and-fluid-resuscitation S5, merge): For a multi-step calculation segment, run the memorised-key trap as try, explain, re-test, then teach the arithmetic chain by fading (worked step, then learner computes each window), and test mechanism and arithmetic as separate probes so one miss diagnoses one wrong model.
- [management] T1 over T8, T4 (surgery/burns-assessment-and-fluid-resuscitation S6, merge): For a titration segment, keep the fast lane and reveal route of the leanest spine, split each paired wrong model into its own feedback node, and give every trap a second probe in a different form (numbers vs sequence, diagnosis vs vignette) instead of lengthening feedback.
- [traps] T10 over T6, T7 (surgery/burns-assessment-and-fluid-resuscitation S7, merge): For a classmate-correction drill segment, take the lean drill spine with a two-form retest per trap, graft the side-by-side contrast items, and write fresh stems for any trap already tested earlier in the module so the segment adds depth instead of replaying S2 or S6.
