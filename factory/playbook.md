# Playbook

What the factory has learned about teaching by conversation. `scripts/factory.py record` appends one line per segment tournament: segment type, winning technique, the techniques it beat, and the editor's lesson. The architect and authors read the recent lines; edit or condense freely, keeping the `- [type]` prefix so authors can find lines for their segment type.

## Starting principles
- [any] Distractors drawn from real PYQ keys beat invented ones: they are the errors learners actually make.
- [any] The first explanation that fails should be followed by a different kind of explanation, not the same one repeated.

## Lessons
- [hook] T9 over T4, T2 (paediatrics/tetralogy-of-fallot S1, merge): For a hook segment, the winner lets the learner fail a real exam stem first and responds with a tempting-then-nudge hint and a retry in a different form, not the answer; put any 'X vs Y' contrast the objective needs on the correct path too, not only on the remediation path.
- [clinical] T2 over T3, T7 (paediatrics/tetralogy-of-fallot S3, merge): For clinical-picture segments, the winner pairs each key vignette with a retry and a second explanation from a different angle, and ends on a multi-trap 'which does NOT fit' that re-tests every trap from the segment at once; check that each option's trap tag matches the trap map's wrong model.
- [diagnosis] T5 over T3, T1 (paediatrics/tetralogy-of-fallot S4, merge): For investigation segments, a faded worked example with a skip lane wins, but the 'why' (mechanism) probe must sit on the shared path before the fork so fast-laners still commit on the causal objective.
- [consolidate] T9 over T12, T8 (paediatrics/tetralogy-of-fallot S8, pick): For consolidation segments, a skip-if-correct fast lane plus a differently-explained rebuild and cold probes beats linear probe sets.
- [management] T5 over T2, T11 (paediatrics/tetralogy-of-fallot S5, pick): For management-sequence segments the variant that tests each step in two forms (first-step, re-asked after a miss, then a cold ordering probe) beats worked-example or error-spotting variants; fix tell runs and dominated distractors on merge.
- [mechanism] T1 over T8, T6 (paediatrics/tetralogy-of-fallot S2, merge): For a mechanism segment, a causal ladder with one committed idea per rung wins; graft a clinical vignette re-test and keep retry lines and hints from naming the answer.
- [management] T4 over T2, T8 (paediatrics/tetralogy-of-fallot S6, merge): For a PYQ-trap recall segment, the winner is the one with a distinct feedback node per distractor naming the wrong model; graft others' retests and gradient foils, and strip uncited 'why tempting' explanations.
- [traps] T6 over T11, T10 (paediatrics/tetralogy-of-fallot S7, pick): For exam-trap segments, a classmate-correction frame with named wrong models beats abstract drill lists, whose distractors are dominated and keys findable by form.
- [hook] T9 over T4, T2 (surgery/acute-intestinal-obstruction S1, merge): For a hook segment on exam-key vs current practice, a both-columns contrast probe plus a skip lane beats plain re-asking; give every trap probe a hint and retry.
- [clinical] T3 over T7, T6 (surgery/acute-intestinal-obstruction S2, merge): For reversed-mechanism segments the winner was the one with a real hint-then-retry ladder naming the tempting model; fixes such as key position, dominated options and a skip lane can be applied by the editor.
